\begin{abstract}
  This research focuses on the development and analysis of
  \textbf{PredCLOCK}, a machine learning-based enhancement to the
  CLOCK cache eviction algorithm. While the standard CLOCK algorithm
  is widely used due to its low overhead, it suffers from the "blind
  promotion" phenomenon, where objects with low reuse probability
  ("one-hit wonders") are unnecessarily promoted, leading to cache
  pollution and wasted write operations. This study aims to mitigate
  these inefficiencies
  by integrating a supervised learning model into the eviction
  decision process to filter promotions based on access patterns
  consistent with Zipfian distributions.
  Simulations were conducted using a custom high-performance
  framework based on \textit{LibCacheSim} under trace replay
  scenarios. The quantitative evaluation
  demonstrates that \textbf{PredCLOCK} achieves a consistent
  reduction in cache promotions of approximately \textbf{8--10\%}
  across various cache sizes compared to the standard CLOCK baseline.
  Furthermore, the system yields an improvement in cache efficiency
  with a reduction in Miss Ratio (up to 0.20\%) in high-pressure
  environments (1\%--20\% cache size). Although the write efficiency
  gains fall short of the theoretical 43\% upper bound established by
  the \textit{Offline CLOCK} oracle, the proposed
  approach successfully optimizes the trade-off between cache hit
  rates and hardware endurance (write reductions) without requiring
  future knowledge.
\end{abstract}
